Observation scope

What this account records

For a dedicated account that joins teams and talks to bots: records every chat, group, private chat and bot it sees, its own messages and button presses, and for each new chat or bot saves its profile and earlier visible messages at once. Review and press Save scope.

Why never your main account ↗

messages · 0 = none

Saved right after the chat appears: earlier messages of a team chat you just joined, the greeting a bot sent before observation noticed it. Messages already saved live are not repeated.

Limits of folder and whole-account modes

Secret chats are end-to-end encrypted between two devices and never reach TgWitness. Nothing is recorded while TgWitness is not running: after a restart it catches up only what Telegram still returns, and the report shows the gap. Chats beyond the chat list limit (1,000 dialogs unless changed in Parameters) are added when they next become active, and the earlier history of an added chat is not fetched. Muted or read state of a folder is not applied: such chats stay observed. Inline bot buttons pressed in another app are invisible to TgWitness; the bot's reply or edit is recorded.

    Full chat export

    Download the entire visible history of one chat as a signed bundle: chat.json (Telegram-style) and a readable chat.html, with an Ed25519 signature so a copy can be checked for changes against your key.     Readable reports

    Report settings

    Language, case details and contents of the report in every signed archive and chat export. Case details are signed into the export; leave a field empty to omit it.

    In the journal report

    Every report is sealed: a signature inside the file covers each of its bytes. Readers check it in the browser by dropping the file onto the report, or with python verify.py report.html --fingerprint …. The archive also carries custody.json and SHA256SUMS.

    Automatic timestamps and an encrypted off-site copy: Extras → Evidence that holds up.

    Verification & limitations

    Your signing identity

    Ed25519 · SHA-256 public-key fingerprint

    Retain this fingerprint and signed checkpoints outside the collector. A self-contained archive can verify its internal consistency; independently retained records help detect replacement or truncation.

    Acquisition process receipt

    The signed journal records program hashes, dependency versions, starting scope, account, settings, run identifier, and periodic heartbeats. Local checkpoints are saved at startup, every five minutes while observing (changeable in Parameters), and on orderly stop.

    This is a collector-signed process record. It is not independent attestation or a trusted timestamp. Copy checkpoints to a separately controlled location for a stronger chain of custody.

    External time anchoring

    Prepare an RFC 3161 request for a signed checkpoint. Submit it to a timestamp authority you trust, then verify its response against independently obtained CA certificates.

    No external service is contacted by this button. A prepared request is not a timestamp proof. The package includes instructions for checking a TSA response with OpenSSL.

    Clear boundaries make stronger evidence

    Message edits

    Versions are preserved when received. Intermediate changes during downtime may be missing. The sender is not necessarily the editor.

    Deletions

    Earlier captured content remains. Telegram usually does not supply the deleter or exact deletion time. Unscoped notices remain account-level observations.

    Reactions & membership

    Counts, recent reactors, service messages, and participant updates are recorded when delivered. These are not guaranteed complete lists.

    Coverage & time

    Collector starts, stops, detected disconnects, and heartbeats are recorded. Brief outages can escape detection. Times depend on the collector's clock.

    Integrity & authenticity

    Signatures bind saved bytes to this collector's key. They do not establish Telegram origin, truthfulness, trusted time, or protection from an operator controlling the signing key.

    Evidence that holds up.

    Timestamps show the journal existed at a given time; an off-site copy keeps it if this machine is lost or seized.

    Evidence mode

    Independent timestamps for this journal

    Sends only the SHA-256 of each signed checkpoint to timestamp services, through this workspace's connection route. Their answers are kept with the checkpoints, included in evidence exports and checked by verify.py.

    Off-site copy

    Copy everything to S3-compatible storage

    The journal, photos & files, checkpoints with their timestamps and chat exports are encrypted on this machine before upload, so the provider stores only encrypted objects. Works with AWS S3 and other S3-compatible services. Download the encryption key and keep it apart: without it the copy cannot be read.

    Collector signs a checkpoint→Witness verifies and signs→Bot publishes to your channel
    
    
    Run the witness on another host, ideally with another operator. Its bot token and signing key stay there. The bot is the publisher; the separate witness process performs verification.
    
  
    
  
    Deployment and verification commands
    
    You need a Telegram bot (create one with @BotFather) that is an administrator of a channel, and this panel's address reachable from the witness — over HTTPS outside a private network. Any Telegram account can watch the channel; its posts carry Telegram's own times.
    
    
    Docker, next to this collector
    
    
    On another server
    
    
    Check it later
    
    
    A running witness records missing or invalid collector answers. Verification compares retained checkpoints with the full export and detects missing or invalid signed posts in a channel export. Witness downtime and posting failures leave intervals without external anchors; signatures do not prove complete Telegram delivery.
    
    
    Witness options
    
    
    How often it checks, how long it waits, which checks it posts (all, only a new head or a problem, only problems) and the words of its posts are options of the witness itself: on its command line, in its environment or in a JSON file given with --config. Wording never affects verification: verify.py checks the signed token at the end of every post.

    2. Enter the repository and a release tag or commit: a commit cannot change later, a branch can.
    3. Check again after every update; the badge shows when the running code is no longer the checked one.
