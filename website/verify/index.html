Check a TgWitness archive

This page checks a TgWitness evidence archive in your browser. The archive is read from your disk and nothing is uploaded: the page is not allowed to open network connections. Nothing inside the archive is used to judge it, not even its own report.

1Key fingerprint from the sender

Ask the sender for the fingerprint of their signing key and get it separately from the archive, for example by phone, in person or in a signed letter. Without it, the page can confirm that the archive is intact, but not whose key signed it.

2The archive

Drop the archive here: the ZIP or ASiC-E file exactly as you received it, or its unpacked folder.

3What this page checks

  • The Ed25519 signature of the manifest, and the key fingerprint against the one from the sender.
  • The SHA-256 of every file in the archive, including the reports and media; no file may be missing, changed or added.
  • For an ASiC-E container: its structure and the SHA-256 values in its manifest.
  • The event journal: the sequence, the hash chain, the hash and the signature of every event, and the count and last hash stated in the signed manifest.

What this page does not check

  • RFC 3161 time stamps, including qualified eIDAS time stamps
  • OpenTimestamps proofs anchored in Bitcoin
  • The CAdES signature of an ASiC-E container (META-INF/signature.p7s)
  • The PAdES signature of report.pdf and the seal inside report.html
  • Intel TDX attestation
  • Witness records

For the full check, start VERIFY.cmd (Windows) or VERIFY.sh (macOS, Linux) from the archive, or run the verifier that comes with it:

python verify.py ARCHIVE.zip --fingerprint SHA256_FROM_THE_SENDER

Without Python, use the portable verifier from TgWitness releases on GitHub: it needs no installation.
