What the comparison demonstrates

Telegram's normal export feature saves available history and selected media for offline use. A process with sufficient access to the storage can change those files. Downloading the official application establishes the application's origin; it does not authenticate the subsequent history of its output.

  2. Native export: the client writes HTML, JSON and selected images.
  3. Disclosed intervention: a local helper deliberately changes selected files.
  4. Comparison: the conversation and altered export appear side by side.

A genuine signature can cover altered input

A console could calculate hashes, sign data and report successful verification even when the input had already been altered. A hash identifies bytes; a signature binds bytes to a key. Neither establishes whether a person actually sent the statement represented by those bytes. See Ed25519 verification.

The acquisition environment, collector key, observation coverage and independent checkpoints matter. TgWitness signatures are collector attestations. Direct API collection does not remove these trust assumptions. Explore the evidence model.

Scope and limits

Is this a Telegram security exploit?

No. The intervention is ordinary local-file modification in a controlled environment. This experiment does not compromise Telegram's servers, alter the official client executable or break its encryption.

Are these complete account exports?

No. The materials cover seven owned test conversations: a personal chat, a bot, a private group, private and public supergroups, and private and public channels. They contain 45 message records. No unrelated chats, contacts or account-wide archive are included.

Are the PDF attachments included?

No. The recorded export settings excluded file attachments. The test messages and images refer to a PDF, but these archives do not contain that PDF. This distinction is preserved in the files and description.

Does the package include the modification tool?

No executables, helper source, transformation configuration, session data, credentials or full working logs are included. The small JavaScript files inside the paired exports are Telegram's unchanged standard HTML-viewer assets, checked to be identical across both versions.

What do the checksums and clock establish?

Checksums identify the exact supplied bytes. They do not independently authenticate the conversation. The video clock is the host's clock; it is not an independent trusted timestamp. The recording's technical metadata also cannot prove the absence of prior editing.

Does API collection remove this trust problem?

Direct collection can reduce reliance on files supplied after the event. TgWitness documents signed observations, hash chains and independently retained checkpoints. These remain collector attestations: their value depends on the acquisition environment, key trust, coverage and independent checks. Read the collection model ↗

What about Telegram Web and mobile apps?

Those are separate planned experiments. This Desktop recording does not establish behavior of mobile storage, backups or Web clients. Read the scoped follow-up plan ↗

This experiment makes no finding about evidence in a particular legal case or the conduct of a particular examiner. Public-source material appearing in the scripted conversations is scene content, not a conclusion of this experiment.

What Forensic Tools Actually Certify

The court pattern we keep seeing

Across the cases and proceedings we have reviewed, the digital evidence chain tends to look like this: a Telegram chat export — HTML or JSON, sometimes captured as a screen recording of a monitor, sometimes a video from an iPhone navigating between profiles — plus an expert conclusion signed by someone with professional credentials. That is the whole chain.

Telegram does not sign its exports. It has never guaranteed their evidentiary weight. The export function exists for data portability. Someone decided the output carries probative value in legal proceedings, and that decision has never been seriously challenged at a technical level. Export Clinic is one data point in that challenge.

The tools are honest. The assumption is not.

We have worked with UFED Cellebrite. It is excellent software. It accurately extracts what is present on a device at the moment of acquisition and produces a cryptographically signed report. That signature certifies the extraction process — not the historical truthfulness of the data. If the device was prepared before it was seized, the report is cryptographically perfect and factually false. The tool is correct. The tool is not a witness.

The same logic applies to Oxygen Forensic Detective, Belkasoft, XRY. We could build a comparison table. We do not see the point. They all do precisely what they describe. None of them are designed to protect against data placed on the device before it was touched. That is not a flaw in the tools — it is a gap in how courts treat their output.

A cryptographic signature on an extraction report certifies the integrity of the extraction. It does not certify the origin or historical accuracy of the extracted data. These are not the same guarantee, and treating them as equivalent is the gap.

The accountability variable

Consider a jurisdiction where forensic tools are procured through public tenders — versions, configurations, acquisition timelines all publicly known — where no regulation requires devices to run current firmware, and where expert witnesses bear no personal criminal liability for the conclusions they sign. Now compare that with the US or UK, where a forensic examiner faces individual accountability before the court for every action taken with evidence.

The tool is identical in both contexts. The accountability structure is not. Remove that accountability and the same instrument that serves justice can serve the opposite purpose — for whoever has access to it, money, and enough lead time.

Technical construction of pre-seizure device state

This section describes what we verified through direct testing on hardware. It is here because the assumption that this would be too difficult is part of the problem.

The firmware window. High-end Samsung devices — Galaxy S Ultra, Z Fold — ship with Knox protection and a locked bootloader. They are marketed as highly secure. What is less visible: the gap between a security patch reaching Samsung's servers and its availability for a specific regional device variant commonly runs to a month. For new models in the first three to four months after release, the window is wider. No law in most jurisdictions requires that a device run current firmware. If applications still function on an older build, there is no visible reason to update — a plausible posture for anyone.

Service-level access. Commercial device service tools operating at the depth of Chimera Tool and comparable products allow low-level interaction during this firmware window without triggering the Knox tamper flags a forensic examiner would look for. The bootloader remains uncompromised.

Constructing the timeline. A full device backup through Samsung Smart Switch is deployed onto an emulator. Script-driven time manipulation and network isolation — MAC address spoofing, certificate binding — are used to build the required event record: messages, calls, application activity. The process runs for as long as needed.

Location data. Cellular tower records are independently verifiable through carrier billing. Leave them alone. Public Wi-Fi access points are the alternative: large technology companies systematically collect and distribute MAC address geolocation data for public infrastructure. A continuous location chain covering any desired period can be assembled from that data without touching a single cell tower log.

Account replacement. Samsung account deletion completes in approximately seven days. Create a new account, restore the prepared backup. The system reports no integrity failures. The bootloader has not been touched. No standard forensic check produces an anomaly.

Telegram specifically. On certain device families, the application database and cache — conversation records, media files, notification metadata, activity timestamps — can be replaced at the OS capability level without obtaining classical root access. Detecting this requires Cellebrite's own specialists examining behavioral divergence in a full dump. That analysis is expensive and rarely requested in routine proceedings.

Other platforms. OnePlus (OxygenOS) works well for bootloader interaction before Android 12. The underlying logic applies across Android variants with platform-specific adjustments. The window exists because architecturally, most Android versions through the recent generations were not designed to enforce data provenance — only data transport security.

Garbage in, garbage out

The extraction report is accurate. The extraction report proves nothing about what was on the device before law enforcement reached it. Expert witnesses who read UFED output and countersign a review are not lying — they are accurately describing what the tool found at acquisition time. The analyst who ran the extraction is not lying. The tool is not lying. No one in the chain is lying. No one in the chain is proving historical truth, either.

This is worth stating carefully because it means the problem cannot be solved by improving the tools or identifying dishonest actors. The problem is a category error in what the output is treated as proving.

The FBI, in international operations, deploys its own field personnel and does not allow parties present at seizure to handle the evidence. This is not distrust of individuals. It is a protocol that ensures the person working the material is personally accountable under law for every action — not merely technically competent, but legally liable. That accountability is what makes evidence evidence.

Zero trust for evidence

In security, trust is the attack surface. In evidence, it appears to be the foundation. We think that is worth examining.

For Telegram exports specifically: do not trust us, do not trust Telegram, do not trust the analyst, do not trust the expert conclusion. Trust the math. If a claim can be verified independently — cryptographically, deterministically, without relying on the reputation of any individual — it is a proof. If it cannot, it is a trust claim. Courts and parties should know which one they are being asked to accept.

On expert witnesses and financial incentives

A lawyer who represents one side in a dispute, then appears in the same proceeding as a neutral third-party expert with compensation linked to their client's outcome — this is a structural contradiction, not an individual character assessment. We are not making accusations. We are asking why the structure is accepted.

The counter-argument we have heard: how much would lying cost him? In practice, in EU jurisdictions with right to erasure, right to be forgotten, and the realistic cost of pursuing expert witness liability — often very little. People who subsequently raise concerns about an incorrect expert conclusion frequently receive legal correspondence asking them to stop. That correspondence is expensive for the person who raised the concern, not for the expert.

A doctoral degree is a record of completed research. It is not a current competence certification for a different field, and it cannot make a mathematically unverifiable claim true by being attached to it. If a credentialed expert wishes to challenge attestations built on SLSA Level 3 provenance, they would need to break the cryptographic signature on the attestation, persuade GitHub's infrastructure to retroactively sign something it did not sign, and rewrite the immutable commit history the attestation references — before it is noticed, with a witness present. If those capabilities exist and have so far been concealed under ordinary difficulty with PDF tooling, we would genuinely find the demonstration interesting.

What the math actually covers

A transaction in a public blockchain proves a mathematical fact: this output was constructed from these inputs under these rules, and the network reached consensus on it. A TLS certificate proves that the domain is controlled by the holder of a specific key. A SLSA Level 3 build attestation produced by GitHub Actions infrastructure references a specific commit hash and workflow run, signed by infrastructure the verifier can check without trusting us.

None of these require trusting an individual. All of them can be re-derived independently. That is what proof means.

Expert signatures with academic credentials cannot be independently verified — they are trust claims. TgWitness collector attestations are also trust claims in certain respects, and we are explicit about where: acquisition environment, collector key, observation coverage, independent checkpoints. The difference is that we describe the trust boundary rather than leaving it implicit.

We examined a range of tools and found no reason to position against any of them. What we observed is a consistent gap between what forensic software certifies and what its output is assumed to prove in legal contexts — a gap that is not technical but categorical. This experiment demonstrates one vector. The math does not care about titles, and the category error does not resolve itself.
