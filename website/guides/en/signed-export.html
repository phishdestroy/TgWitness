In short. A signature tells you who sent a file. It cannot tell you whether the file is complete, correct or true. Only checks on the bytes can, and anyone can repeat them.

Zero trust: check the file, not the sender

Zero trust means that nobody has to be believed: not a firm, not a title, not a signature, and not us. An answer to an access request should prove itself. It has to answer three questions, and a signature answers none of them.

Is the copy complete?Does the requester have everything that is theirs, and is every omission counted and explained?
Is the copy unchanged?Is each record what was originally held, and would any later change show?
Who decided what is hidden?Was the withholding decided by someone entitled to decide, and can anyone check it afterwards?

Most legal disputes in the EU stay closed: settlements under NDA, sealed files, decisions that are never published. We have nothing against that. This case is different, and probably not because what happened in it is rare. It has no NDA and no court decision, so it can be read from end to end: a very simple law, the GDPR; one request for a person's own data; what was actually delivered; and black rectangles that anyone can remove.

What makes it unique is who answered, and how. Dr. Tobias Schelinski and Dr. Patrick Zurheide are partners of Taylor Wessing, in its own words “a European legal services powerhouse”. Both hold doctorates; one is a certified specialist lawyer for IT law, the other holds a Master of Laws from Aberdeen. They took 106 days not to process a lawful access request. Along the way they handed the reported account holder the complaint texts and logins of more than 1,800 other people, some of whom may be minors, under black rectangles that anyone can remove.

PhishDestroy's view. A login, the user name, is highly personal data. It is reused across services, and an account that has lived for years is, in most cases, tied to a real person who can be identified through it. Looking only at the fifty visible fragments, we think any court could indeed be misled. With the full picture, the timeline and the arithmetic on this page make it plain that the GDPR request was not processed. No signature and no misspelled name changes that fact.

What the signature can and cannot tell you

A qualified electronic signature is strong evidence about the sender. It is not evidence about the content. The two questions have to be checked separately.

✓A signature can show

  • who signed the document
  • that the signed bytes have not changed since signing

!A signature cannot show

  • that the export is complete
  • that the redaction removed anything
  • that the statements in it are true
  • that anyone read it before signing

The model: nobody decides alone, nothing can be swapped

This is not about one piece of software. It is about who decides. In this case two lawyers decided which fifty complaints mattered and that the rest did not. No court, no regulator.

Some things must stay hidden: the reporters' logins are other people's data. The question is who decides, and whether anyone can check the decision afterwards. A black rectangle answers neither, and anyone can remove it. Two standard tools answer both: a salted hash in the requester's copy, and the original sealed for the public key of a supervisory authority or a court.

SALTED COMMITMENT · ONE PER WITHHELD FIELD

c = SHA-256(salt ‖ value)

The salt is 16 random bytes, new for every field. The requester's copy carries c in place of the value, under the firm's signature. The value and its salt travel in an envelope encrypted for the authority's public key, with OpenPGP or with X25519 and AES-GCM. Without the authority's private key nobody can open it, and the firm cannot swap a value without breaking the commitment it signed.

  1. Step 1 · the requesterHands over the copy and the envelope

    The signed copy with its commitments and the sealed envelope go to the supervisory authority or the court.

  2. Step 2 · the authorityOpens the envelope and recomputes

    The inspector decrypts with the authority's private key and recomputes SHA-256(salt ‖ value) for every field. Each result must equal the commitment the firm signed.

  3. Step 3 · an independent bodyDecides what was really protected

    The authority, not the firm, reads everything that was withheld, here 1,944 complaint texts and a security history, and decides whether third-party rights required withholding it.

Nobody decides aloneThe firm still proposes what to withhold. Someone with the full picture and no stake in the dispute checks it.
Nothing leaksThe requester sees no other person's data. The salt makes a commitment useless for guessing: without it, a one-word complaint could be found by hashing a word list.
Nothing can be swappedEvery value is bound to the commitment the firm signed. A different text, or a missing one, fails the check.
The signature means somethingA qualified signature over the commitments binds the firm to every hidden value, not only to the cover letter.
Titles do not matterThe check is arithmetic. It needs the copy, the envelope and the authority's key, not anyone's word.

None of this needs new infrastructure. German supervisory authorities already publish OpenPGP keys: the Federal Commissioner for Data Protection lists one for every unit, and the LfDI Baden-Württemberg offers its public key for encrypted e-mail. German courts already receive filings through the EGVP, whose end-to-end encryption is based on the OSCI protocol.

PhishDestroy's position. Who gave counsel the right to decide what is significant and what is not? Lawyers should not make that decision. Everything should be handed over: not under black rectangles that anyone can remove, but sealed for the key of a court or a regulator, so that there is no room for “errors”, for “misunderstandings”, or for showing only what is convenient.

The honest model: full data, a declared rule, the key with the authority

The requester gets everything that is not in dispute, at once. Anything withheld is not covered with a drawing and not kept back in silence. It goes, complete and signed, encrypted to the key of the authority that is entitled to decide on it.

The authority does not need trust either. Art. 58(1)(e) GDPR already empowers a supervisory authority to obtain access to all personal data a controller processes. The model only hands it over in a form that can be verified on arrival.

The reasons given in the letters, ownership doubts, payments by third parties, complaints, suspected phishing, explain why Art. 15 was not fulfilled. They do not fulfil it. Data access is a right of the data subject, not a bargaining chip in someone else's dispute.

What the software is built to guarantee

Integrity of the dataEvery record is hashed into a chain and signed with Ed25519. The verifier recomputes every record, so any change shows.
Delivery you can checkArchives travel encrypted for their recipient; large ones in encrypted parts with a signed manifest, assembled and verified locally.
Other people's data stays closedA sharing copy withholds by rule and names what it withheld. Revealing a masked value in the panel is audited.
No room for errorThe checks are arithmetic: the file, a trusted key fingerprint and a retained checkpoint. Nobody's word is needed.
No room for manipulationEdits and deletions become new records, a shortened journal fails against a retained checkpoint, and a disclosure never looks complete when it is not.

A typo in a name does not change data signed by the software. A month past a deadline does not change the original records. Any change breaks the chain, and the verifier says so. Try both below.

Our position

PhishDestroy's position. We respect the law and the courts. The law is not an opponent of the truth. Those who bend it in order not to comply with it are no allies of a court, and a doctoral prefix cannot be an authority for one.

A doctorate is a historical fact: one piece of research in a narrow theoretical field, defended once. It does not make anyone an IT lawyer.
