Reference / Evidence

Evidence and trust

What exports, video, web archives and signed records can establish, and how the collecting device affects their reliability.

An export, screenshot or recording can be useful evidence. Its reliability depends on how it was collected, preserved and corroborated. A valid hash or signature checks the saved material; it does not independently establish that the collecting device received truthful source content. This boundary also applies to TgWitness.

This guide separates technical verification from legal assessment. Product documentation and the legal provisions below were reviewed on 1 November 2026; the Hunchly comparison describes its published documentation, not a new product audit.

Where trust enters the capture#

The threat model here assumes an attacker or operator can alter the collection environment before the evidence is committed. This is a conditional analysis, not a claim that a particular device or product has been compromised.

An official client and an encrypted connection help protect normal use. They do not make the surrounding operating system, browser or display independently trustworthy. If content is substituted after decryption but before capture, the exporter can save it accurately and the signer can sign it correctly. Neither operation needs a broken hash algorithm or a forged signature.

A video made on the same device can record the same substituted presentation. A separate camera can confirm what the display showed, but the display alone does not authenticate what Telegram supplied. Likewise, matching HTML, JSON, screenshots and browser logs from one controlled environment may share the same failure. Their agreement is useful context; it is not automatically independent corroboration.

What each form of evidence contributes#

Material Useful contribution Remaining question
Telegram Desktop export Readable or machine-readable conversations and selected media Who controlled the client and export files, and what was available or excluded?
Screenshot or screen recording Visible context, sequence of actions and presentation Does the presentation faithfully reflect the source, and what happened outside the recording?
Local web archive, including MHTML Page content and associated resources for later examination What did the browser supply to the archiver, and who controlled that browser?
Collector-signed archive Changes to signed bytes can be detected relative to a trusted key and retained reference Who controlled the signing key and the input before signing?
Independently retained checkpoint or verified timestamp A retained hash or checkpoint for comparison with a later submission Which bytes and boundary are covered, and what does the independent party actually attest?

Telegram documents its Desktop export as an offline HTML/JSON copy and its Takeout API as a way to retrieve selected account data. These descriptions do not provide a Telegram signature over the final exported archive for a recipient to verify. Our conclusion is limited to those documented export mechanisms; it is not a claim that Telegram evidence has no legal value. Telegram Desktop export, Takeout API.

The same distinction applies to background requests, cache entries and network logs. They may reveal inconsistencies and help corroborate a collection. Their complexity alone does not establish who produced them. An independently operated archive or separately obtained service record introduces a different source to compare, subject to its own collection and custody limitations.

Hunchly illustrates the same boundary#

Hunchly's documentation describes SHA-256 hashing of MHTML captures, photos and attachments, with independent hash verification. It also explains why an altered file accompanied by a newly calculated hash can pass that comparison, and points to signatures and source corroboration as additional checks. Hunchly: content, photo and attachment hashing.

Its published Evidence Guide, revision 1.0 of 17 October 2018, describes locally generated GPG keys and signatures for MHTML captures. It explicitly identifies access to the local private key as a way to sign modified material again. This is a documented trust limitation, not evidence of an undisclosed current exploit. The guide also says Chrome performs the MHTML capture. Describing Hunchly as relying only on an investigator's reputation would omit these technical controls. Hunchly Evidence Guide, sections 2–4.

Whether material collected with a particular product has been admitted in a case requires the relevant decision and circumstances. We make no claim here about how frequently courts accept Hunchly or TgWitness, or about either product having a general court approval.

What TgWitness adds, and where its protection stops#

TgWitness records observations in a signed, linked journal and provides exports that a recipient can check. Retained checkpoints, timestamps and a separate witness give reviewers references beyond the collector's present files. They help expose changes to previously committed records. Coverage records and explicit collection outcomes help explain what was and was not captured. Evidence format and handoff, collection scope.

The time of a possible substitution matters:

  1. After signing, without access to the signing key: changing a signed record or manifest-bound file fails verification with a trusted verifier and key.
  2. With the collector key, after an independent checkpoint was retained: a replacement journal can have valid new signatures, but a changed prefix fails comparison with that earlier checkpoint. A checkpoint kept only on the same controlled device can be replaced along with the journal. Records beyond the retained boundary need their own coverage.
  3. Before a checkpoint covering an observation is independently retained: false input can be signed and anchored successfully, including new records appended after an earlier checkpoint. The resulting checkpoint or timestamp still covers those false records; it does not establish their truth.

A TgWitness witness receives the collector's signed checkpoints; it does not independently read the underlying Telegram conversation. Its key and retained records must be trusted separately. When the entire submitted journal must be witnessed, use the handoff procedure requiring the final export boundary. An ordinary integrity pass can still report an unwitnessed tail.

A verified RFC 3161 response can support that a checkpoint existed by the authority's stated time. It does not establish when a Telegram message was sent or whether its content was true. A prepared request or an included response file alone is insufficient. When verifying an export with tgwitness.verify, checking included TSA signatures requires an explicitly trusted CA through --tsa-ca. OpenTimestamps receipts need independent ots verify, with proof upgrades where necessary. Timestamp procedure, RFC 3161.

In GitHub Actions capture, a temporary hosted runner collects the messages. Attestation binds the archive to an approved workflow. This mode still trusts GitHub, the approved software and dependencies, the helper/browser and the tunnel. It is not confidential hardware, a Telegram signature, or proof that nobody accessed the host. A local signed run receipt is also a self-reported measurement, not hardware attestation.

Legal weight depends on the foundation#

Four questions should remain distinct: whether the item is what it is said to be, whether the submitted bytes match the retained record, whether the content supports the claimed facts, and whether applicable rules permit its use. A cryptographic check addresses only part of that foundation.

In US federal proceedings, Rule 901 provides ways to authenticate evidence, including knowledgeable witness testimony and evidence about a process or system. Rules 902(13) and 902(14) provide certification routes for certain electronic records and copies, with procedural requirements. Their committee notes distinguish authentication from issues such as hearsay, relevance and attribution. A software signature alone is not the qualified person's certification described there. Federal Rule of Evidence 901, Federal Rule of Evidence 902.

Where Canada's Evidence Act applies, section 31.1 addresses authentication of electronic documents. Section 31.2 links the best evidence rule to system integrity or an applicable presumption; section 31.3 specifies circumstances for a presumption of integrity. These are defined evidentiary conditions, not a blanket presumption that any licensed investigator or commercial product is infallible. Canada Evidence Act, 31.1, 31.2, 31.3.

These examples do not settle admissibility in another jurisdiction or a particular case. The technical checks should be applied consistently to material supporting or challenging an allegation. This guide makes no claim that prosecution evidence is generally easier to admit than defence evidence.

Prepare a record another person can assess#

  • Describe the account, collection device, operator, software version, access rights, selected scope and collection period. Record interruptions and possible compromise instead of treating a successful signature check as a clean-device test.
  • Keep original packages and record transfers, hashes and the source of time. Retain the key fingerprint and checkpoints through a separately trusted channel. Verify the actual witness or timestamp coverage before describing it in a report.
  • Seek independent corroboration appropriate to the claim: another participant's records, an independent capture or lawfully obtained service records. Explain their independence and limitations; agreement between copies is not enough by itself.
  • Preserve relevant context. Identify omitted files, unavailable history and redactions. Prepare a separate minimized disclosure when needed, and describe its transformations without presenting it as the full original.
  • Give the recipient verification instructions and the material those checks need. Keep observations, technical findings and the analyst's conclusions distinguishable.

For a particular handoff, state the archive hash, trusted key, verification result and the exact externally covered boundary. Describe source authenticity, account attribution, completeness and legal conclusions separately. This gives the next reviewer specific claims to examine.
